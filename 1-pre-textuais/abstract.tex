Sudden cardiac death (SCD) is one of the main causes of death in patients
with heart failure (HF). This work aims to predict the occurrence of SCD in
a population of HF patients, over a four-year follow-up period, based on
parameters extracted from the 24-hour Holter electrocardiogram (ECG) and on
machine learning models. The population comprised 517 patients in sinus
rhythm from the public MUSIC database: 55 who died of SCD and 462 who
survived four years. Three scenarios were evaluated in stratified five-fold
cross-validation with patient-level splits, by the area under the ROC curve
(AUC) for SCD prediction, with 95\,\% confidence intervals (CI). In scenario
(I), deep neural networks --- convolutional, residual (ResNet-18 to
ResNet-50) and attention networks --- processed the original ECG (raw
signal). In scenario (II), the signal underwent preprocessing and feature
extraction --- heart rate variability (SDNN, RMSSD, LF and HF power,
Poincaré and entropy), ectopy and beat morphology (QRS, QT and P and T
waves) ---, used in classical machine learning models: L1- and L2-penalized
logistic regression, principal component analysis and random forest. In
scenario (III), for comparison and analysis, models based on clinical
variables were evaluated --- patient data (age, sex, NYHA class), ECG
reports, Holter measurements and laboratory and imaging exams, such as
Pro-BNP and echocardiography ---, alone and combined with the ECG data.
Scenario (I) yielded AUC between 0.48 and 0.56; the best network, the
minimal ResNet-18, reached 0.562 (CI: 0.48--0.64). Scenario (II) yielded AUC
between 0.58 and 0.62 with 44 markers and 0.647 (CI: 0.56--0.73) with two
markers chosen from the literature, the variation of heart rate over the day
and the ectopic burden. In scenario (III), six clinical variables reached AUC
0.661 (CI: 0.58--0.74); combined with the ECG markers, 0.681 (CI:
0.60--0.76); and combined with the exams, 0.733 (CI: 0.66--0.80).
Performance gains were observed, although not statistically significant,
from models based on parameters extracted from the ECG, especially those
related to heart rate, over deep neural networks that take the raw signal as
input, and from combining clinical variables with ECG data over clinical
variables alone.

\textbf{Keywords:} Sudden Cardiac Death. Holter ECG. Deep Learning. Heart Rate
Variability. Heart Failure. Risk Stratification.
