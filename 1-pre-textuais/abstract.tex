Sudden cardiac death (SCD) is one of the main causes of death in patients
with heart failure, and identifying those at risk guides prevention. The objective of this
work is to identify which machine learning approach best predicts, from the
24-hour Holter electrocardiogram (ECG), which patients with chronic heart
failure will suffer SCD: deep learning models applied to the raw signal,
models based on markers extracted by ECG processing, or their combination
with clinical variables. Prediction was
framed as a patient-level binary classification: distinguishing the 55
patients who died of SCD from the 462 who survived four years of follow-up,
among 517 patients in sinus rhythm from the public MUSIC database
(PhysioNet). The three scenarios were compared in stratified five-fold cross-validation with patient-level splits, by the area under the ROC curve (AUC) for SCD prediction, with 95\,\% confidence intervals (CI). In the first, deep learning models ---
convolutional, residual (ResNet-18 to ResNet-50) and attention networks ---
were trained directly on the raw Holter signal, in 15 configurations. In the
second, feature learning, markers extracted by digital ECG processing from
5-minute and 30-second segments --- heart rate variability (SDNN, RMSSD, LF
and HF power, Poincaré and entropy), ectopy (ectopic burden, couplets and
pauses) and beat morphology (QRS, QT and P and T waves) --- were
the input of machine learning models: L1- and L2-penalized logistic regression, principal component analysis and
random forest. In the third, six clinical variables (age, sex, NYHA class, Q
waves, non-sustained ventricular tachycardia and paroxysmal supraventricular
tachyarrhythmia) were used alone, with the
markers and, in fusion networks, with the signal. Deep learning predicted SCD
with AUC between 0.48 and 0.56. The markers reached AUC between 0.58 and 0.62
with 44 markers and 0.647 (CI: 0.56--0.73) with two markers defined from the
literature before the analysis, the variation of heart rate over the day and
the ectopic burden, 0.085 above the best network (0.562). The clinical
variables predicted SCD with AUC 0.661 (CI: 0.58--0.74); combined with the
markers they reached 0.681 (CI: 0.60--0.76), and with the fusion networks at
most 0.579. Thus, ECG processing yielded
better SCD predictors than deep learning on the raw signal, and the best
models combine the ECG markers with the clinical variables.

\textbf{Keywords:} Sudden Cardiac Death. Holter ECG. Deep Learning. Heart Rate
Variability. Heart Failure. Risk Stratification.
