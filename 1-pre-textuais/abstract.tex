This work investigates the prediction of sudden cardiac death in patients with
chronic heart failure from 24-hour Holter electrocardiography (ECG). Data were
obtained from the MUSIC database, available on the PhysioNet platform,
comprising three orthogonal lead signals (X, Y and Z) sampled at 200~Hz and
clinical variables from 992 patients followed at Spanish university
hospitals. A case-control design restricted to patients in sinus rhythm was
adopted, in which cases are deaths from sudden cardiac death within four
years and controls are survivors with complete four-year follow-up, for a
total of 517 individuals and 55 events. Four approaches were compared under
the same protocol of stratified five-fold cross-validation, with
patient-level splits, decision threshold set on internal validation and
bootstrap confidence intervals. The first applies deep neural networks
directly to the signal --- convolutional, residual, attention-based and fusion networks ---, in nineteen
configurations with 14 thousand to 16.5 million parameters. The second
extracts markers from the signal --- heart rate variability, beat morphology
and ectopy --- from 5-minute and 30-second segments. The third uses clinical variables only. In the fourth, a network learns to
predict echocardiographic, ECG and laboratory attributes, and its
representation is used for prediction. The neural networks
achieved areas under the ROC curve (AUC) between 0.48 and 0.56, compatible with chance. The markers reached AUC between 0.59 and 0.62, and the clinical
variables 0.66 (95\,\% CI: 0.58--0.74). Combining clinical variables with
markers, with selection performed within each fold, reached 0.68 (95\,\% CI:
0.60--0.76), close to values reported in the literature for the same
database, but the gain over clinical variables alone was not statistically
significant. The representation of the network guided by clinical
attributes reached 0.68 (95\,\% CI: 0.60--0.76) on its own, equivalent to
clinical variables, but added no information to the measured exams, whose
best model, with echocardiography, reached 0.73. The results indicate that, with the available number of events,
no predictive pattern of sudden cardiac death could be learned from the raw
signal by training the network directly on the outcome, that the prognostic
information in the signal overlaps with that of the available exams, and
that higher estimates depend on evaluation choices --- variable
selection on the whole cohort, single data splits, treating segments as
independent observations and inclusion of non-sinus rhythms ---, whose
effects this work quantifies on the same patients.

\textbf{Keywords:} Sudden Cardiac Death. Holter ECG. Deep Learning. Heart Rate
Variability. Heart Failure. Risk Stratification.
