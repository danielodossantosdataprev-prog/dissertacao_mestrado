This work evaluates machine learning models to predict, from the 24-hour
Holter electrocardiogram (ECG), which patients with chronic heart failure
will suffer sudden cardiac death (SCD) within four years. Prediction was
framed as a patient-level binary classification: distinguishing the 55
patients who died of SCD from the 462 who survived the four-year follow-up,
in a cohort of 517 patients in sinus rhythm from the public MUSIC database
(PhysioNet). Three scenarios were tested in stratified
five-fold cross-validation with patient-level splits. The main metric is the area under
the ROC curve (AUC) for SCD prediction, with 95\,\% bootstrap confidence intervals (CI). In the first scenario, deep
learning models --- convolutional, residual (ResNet-18 to ResNet-50),
attention and fusion networks with clinical variables --- were trained directly on the raw Holter signal to
predict SCD, in 19 configurations. In the second, models based on ECG processing techniques
extracted from the signal, in 5-minute and 30-second segments, heart rate
variability parameters (SDNN, RMSSD, LF and HF power, Poincaré and entropy), ectopy parameters (ectopic burden, couplets and pauses) and
beat morphology parameters (QRS duration, QT interval and P and T waves),
used in L1- and L2-penalized logistic regression,
principal component analysis and random forest. In the third, six clinical
variables (age, sex, NYHA functional class, Q waves, non-sustained
ventricular tachycardia and paroxysmal supraventricular tachyarrhythmia)
were used alone and combined with the ECG parameters. The deep learning
models predicted SCD with AUC between 0.48 and 0.56. The models based on ECG
processing reached AUC between 0.58 and 0.62 with 44 parameters and 0.647
(CI: 0.56--0.73) with two parameters defined from the literature before the
analysis, the variation of heart rate over the day and the ectopic burden,
0.085 above the best deep learning network (0.562). The clinical
variables predicted SCD with AUC 0.661 (CI: 0.58--0.74), and their
combination with the ECG parameters reached 0.681 (CI: 0.60--0.76). With the study
exams, such as echocardiography and Pro-BNP, the clinical variables reached
0.733 (CI: 0.66--0.80), the best result. Thus, ECG
processing techniques yielded better SCD predictors than deep learning on the
raw signal, and the best models combine the ECG parameters with the clinical
variables and exams.

\textbf{Keywords:} Sudden Cardiac Death. Holter ECG. Deep Learning. Heart Rate
Variability. Heart Failure. Risk Stratification.
