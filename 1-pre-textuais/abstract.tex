
This work investigates the prediction of sudden cardiac death in patients with
chronic heart failure from 24-hour Holter electrocardiography (ECG). Data were
obtained from the MUSIC database, available on the PhysioNet platform,
comprising three orthogonal lead signals (X, Y and Z) sampled at 200~Hz, along
with clinical and demographic variables from 992 patients followed at Spanish
university hospitals between 2003 and 2008. A case-control design was adopted,
in which cases correspond to documented sudden cardiac death and controls to
survivors with complete four-year follow-up, restricted to patients in sinus
rhythm, totalling 517 individuals and 55 events. Three modelling strategies
were compared under the same evaluation protocol. The first applies deep
learning directly to the raw signal, covering eleven configurations across
convolutional, residual, recurrent and attention-based networks, with capacity
ranging from 109 thousand to 16 million parameters, including Bayesian
hyperparameter search. The second extracts signal-derived markers --- heart
rate variability, spectral analysis and morphological measures --- sampled in
five-minute windows at hourly intervals, which also allows characterising how
these markers vary throughout the day. The third uses clinical variables only.
All models were evaluated by five-fold stratified cross-validation with
patient-level partitioning, decision thresholds defined on the validation
split, and confidence intervals estimated by bootstrap resampling. Models
applied to the raw signal achieved areas under the ROC curve between 0.48 and
0.56, with confidence intervals compatible with chance in every configuration.
Extracted markers reached 0.62 (95\% CI: 0.54--0.71) when reduced by principal
component analysis, a procedure that does not use outcome information, and the
best overall performance was obtained by combining six clinical variables with
two heart rate variability markers (0.69; 95\% CI: 0.61--0.77). The incremental
gain of ECG over clinical variables alone was not statistically demonstrable.
The results indicate that, in cohorts with a small number of events,
signal-processing feature extraction outperforms representation learning from
raw signals, and that the available statistical power limits the detection of
clinically realistic effect sizes.


\textbf{Keywords:} Sudden Cardiac Death. Holter ECG. Deep Learning. Heart Rate
Variability. Heart Failure. Risk Stratification.
